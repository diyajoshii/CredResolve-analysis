Collections Recovery — Audit of the Reported Improvement

2026-01-01 → 2026-08-08 · 220 days · 30,000 accounts · ₹1,049 Cr book

The claim
“Recovery has improved by 11% month-on-month.”

Recovery has been flat all year. The 11% is February.

Net recovery has run at ₹0.555 Cr per day every month, varying by 2.0%. A trend test returns p = 0.11 — no trend, and the point estimate is negative. Calls, contacts, promises and payments per day are equally flat.

The only +11% anywhere in the data is the February → March step, and it is the difference between a 28-day month and a 31-day one.

What the audit found

Fourteen data issues; these six change a decision.
FindingGradeWhat the data shows
Targeting carries no information Fact 43.1% of targeting aims at accounts last recorded CLOSED, WRITEOFF or PAID — against a 42.9% random-selection baseline. Priority score, channel, risk segment and DPD band are all independent of who pays. This is the one genuine gap in the operation.
Calls outside the legal window Compliance 45.9% of calls fall outside 08:00–21:00 IST (RBI Fair Practices Code). Invisible today because event_at is a naive local string and the timezone sits in a separate column with three values.
Promises undercounted by half Overstated Dashboards match 'PTP' and report an 11.2% PTP rate. PTP and PROMISE_TO_PAY are the same event under two live taxonomies; the true rate is 22.4%.
Agent identity is unrecoverable Unmeasurable 1,000 agent IDs carry 1,099 employee codes in a single connected graph; each ID shows ~9.5 different names. Resolving on employee code — the obvious fix — invents a +56% productivity gain. All agent and tenure metrics are withdrawn.
Channel conversion is a dial Unmeasurable Only 3% of payments have any interaction within 24 hours. Last-touch share barely moves across a 365× change in attribution window, because credit follows message volume, not effect.
Cost data does not exist Absent No cost, rate, tariff, salary or commission column in any of the 17 tables. Cost per ₹ recovered — a headline metric — is not computable. Every ROI figure below rests on stated assumptions.
The ₹10 Cr decision

Stage the commitment. Spend ₹1.2 Cr to make the decision answerable.

None of the six options survives contact with the evidence. Better targeting is the only one where the data proves a gap rather than merely failing to disprove one — but a proven gap is not a proven payoff. Fund the measurement, then let the result release the capital.

Question one for the CFO

Is this book owned or collected for a fee? On a 20% agency fee, break-even on ₹10 Cr rises from a +4.9% lift to +25% — a lever this data gives no reason to believe exists. The answer changes the recommendation to an unambiguous no.

The limit of this evidence

The minimum detectable effect of this dataset is 12.9%. Even had the 11% improvement been real, this data could not have seen it. The claim sits below the resolution of the instrument used to make it.
